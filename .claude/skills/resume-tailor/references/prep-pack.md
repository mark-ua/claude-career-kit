# Preparation pack (`prep.md`)

Written for the candidate, in their language. It should be usable the evening before
an interview: scannable, prioritized, with links.

## Template

```markdown
# <Role> @ <Company> - preparation

Vacancy: <url> · Saved: <date> · Match: <score>/100 · Verdict: <apply / with caveats / skip>

## 1. Learn (gaps)
| Priority | Topic | Why they need it | Minimum to be credible | Resource | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | ... | quote from the posting | what to be able to explain or do | 1-2 links | ~3 h |

## 2. Refresh (you know it, but will be asked)
- Topic - what exactly to revisit, and which of your own cases illustrates it

## 3. Your stories for this role
| Their requirement | Your story (STAR in one line) | Number to mention |

## 4. Company in brief
- What they do / for whom / business model
- Size, stage, funding or owner, HQ, where the team sits
- Product and tech: stack, release cadence, anything public about quality/testing
- Recent news (last 6-12 months)
- What employees say (source + date)
- Why this role is open (if known or inferable - mark inference as such)

## 5. Founder / CEO
- Name, role, background in 3-4 lines
- What they say publicly about product, engineering, AI, culture (with links)
- One or two things worth referencing in conversation

## 6. Likely interview format and topics
- Stages if known (recruiter screen → technical → hiring manager → ...)
- Topics by stage, drawn from the posting and the research

## 7. Questions to ask them
- 5-7 questions that show understanding of their situation (not "what is the culture")

## 8. Risks and red flags
- Eligibility, contract, overtime hints, vague posting, bad reviews - each with source

## 9. Money
- Stated range, or market range for this role/location with source; the candidate's
  target from candidate.md; a suggested answer to "what are your expectations?"

## Sources
- numbered list of URLs used
```

## Guidance

- **Learn** is for real gaps only, ordered by how likely the topic is to decide the
  interview. For each, define the minimum that makes the candidate credible: usually
  "explain the concept, compare it with the tool you know, and have done one small
  hands-on exercise". Keep the list short enough to actually do - 3-6 items; move the
  rest to a "later" line.
- **Refresh** covers things the candidate has but may be rusty on, especially
  fundamentals senior people are still asked about.
- **Stories** map their top requirements to the candidate's real cases from the master
  resume. This table is the backbone of the interview answers.
- Company and founder sections: facts with sources; if nothing reliable was found
  about something, write "not found" instead of filling in.
- Keep the whole file to roughly two screens. Depth belongs in links.
